# Akọni & Dike brief, part 5: SFX, music, voice and language

Binding: `CULTURAL_GUIDE.md` sections 3 to 5, `ASSET_BIBLE.md` "Audio", `STYLE_GUIDE.md` section 9 (SFX rules: attack 2 to 5 ms, exponential release, peak <= -6 dBFS, +/-2% pitch and +/-1 dB random per trigger, soft and full mixes, **no generic blip, coin or whoosh**). Cues are <= 300 ms; fanfare-class cues (marked **L**) are <= 1.5 s. Code lives in `games/akoni-dike/src/audio/sfx.ts` (`akoniSfx`) and `games/akoni-dike/src/audio/instruments-akoni.ts` (new voices, registered with `audio.registerInstruments`, so **core is not edited**). Register `{ ...coreSfx, ...akoniSfx }`; soft mix via `softSfx`.

## 1. Sound identity per campaign

The sound sets differ on purpose so each campaign sounds like its people: **Kiriji (Yoruba) = talking drum (gángan, dùndún), agogo, with shekere (and crowd) where it fits**; **Ekumeku (Igbo) = ogene, udu, ekwe**; **Prologue ch00 (Ife legend) = the Kiriji set plus the torch and crowd everyday sounds** (soft drum, shekere crowd). No violence uses a flesh or blood sound: a hit is wood and drum.

**The rule, stated once and tested (QA item 43).** Every battle, order, stealth and reward cue exists as a `K` variant (Kiriji, and ch00) and an `E` variant (Ekumeku). The game picks the suffix from the campaign. Cues are never shared across campaigns, except the **universal** cues in section 3.2 (guide-mapped taps and museum UI), which may use any core voice. The machine-checkable list (cue name to allowed voices) is in section 3.1. Nothing in the Kiriji set uses `ekwe`, `udu` or `ogene`; nothing in the Ekumeku set uses `talkingDrum*`, `agogo*` or `shekere*`.

## 3. SFX map: event to instrument or everyday sound to preset

Preset names are `akoniSfx.<name>`. `K` = Kiriji set (also ch00), `E` = Ekumeku set. Cues <= 300 ms unless marked **L** (<= 1.5 s).

```json
  "universalCues": ["tap", "select", "back", "menu", "move", "tick", "start", "success", "fail", "factSlide", "sourceCard", "codexOpen"],

```

Test rules (A1, `sfx.test.ts`): (1) a cue whose name ends in `K` may use only `sets.K` plus `everyday` voices; (2) a cue ending in `E` only `sets.E` plus `everyday`; (3) a cue in `universalCues` only `sets.U`; (4) a cue in `exceptions` only the listed voices (plus, for the horns, nothing else); (5) no cue uses a `forbiddenEverywhere` voice; (6) the game's UI and engine never call a `forbiddenCoreCues` name (a source grep); (7) every `K` or `E` cue in the map has its partner unless it appears in the single-campaign list in section 3.3; (8) length <= 300 ms or marked **L** <= 1.5 s; (9) a cue made only of `everyday` voices must be `smokeBedE`. A cue that matches none of the rules fails the test.

## 4. Music brief (commissioned; silent placeholders until delivered)

Rules (as `MUSIC_BRIEF.md`): original compositions only, work-for-hire or fully licensed for web-game embedding; instruments Nigerian and live or clearly licensed; **instrumental only**; no samples of existing songs; no artist sound-alikes; no pan-African "savanna" bed; no AI music of unknown licence. -16 LUFS, seamless. **Format and budget:** ship **one format per loop**: `.ogg` (Vorbis) is preferred; `.mp3` is added only if a real-device test shows an Android WebView that cannot decode `.ogg`, and then it replaces the `.ogg` for that device class in the manifest rather than being loaded as well. Budgets count the one shipped format (shipping both would double the music bytes). **Size:** max loop length = 300 KB x 8 / bitrate, i.e. about 25 s at 96 kbps or about 38 s at 64 kbps; `MUSIC_BRIEF.md`'s 30 to 60 s at 96 kbps (360 to 720 KB) cannot meet the 300 KB rule. This game asks **24 to 33 s loops at <= 64 kbps joint stereo (about 200 to 265 KB)**; every slot below is within that. Leave 800 Hz to 2.5 kHz clear on cue beats so `tell`, `parry` and `padPerfect` stay audible. Duck music 6 dB under barks. Placeholders are silent `.mp3` files with the final names until the format is decided. Files sit in `A/packs/audio-kiriji/`, `A/packs/audio-ekumeku/` and `A/packs/ch06|ch16/audio/`.

## 5. Voice barks and dialogue

**All story text is read on screen, not voiced** `[AB]`. Only short battle and stealth barks are recorded: <= 1.2 s, three takes (neutral, brighter, softer), mono 44.1 kHz WAV master, delivered as one format (`.ogg` preferred, same rule as music) at 48 kbps, peak -3 dBFS, 150 ms room tone at the head. Barks are <= 150 KB per campaign (part 4). One voice at a time; music ducks 6 dB. Recorded by **Nigerian voice actors; Yoruba and Igbo lines by native speakers who also confirm spelling and tone marks; never text-to-speech, never a faked or exaggerated accent**, no "villager" voices. The rival side speaks in the same register as the heroes, never mocking. **Company constables speak plain English in a neutral voice with no ethnic accent signal** `[SC 3]`. Licence (buyout, with a no-AI-training clause welcome) and actor go in `CREDITS.md`. ## 6. Language, diacritics and the name registry

Dialogue is English with local terms plus Pidgin banter; `en` and `pcm` ship from day one, `yo` and `ig` later `[CLAUDE.md]`. Rules: write strings in NFC; keep every Yoruba, Igbo or other-language word **tagged `_review: true`** until a native speaker signs it; never guess tone marks; check Bungee and Nunito coverage with `packages/core/assets/tools/font-coverage.py` and check `Ẹ́`, `ọ̀`, `ṣ́`-type stacks visually on a real phone (`STYLE_GUIDE.md` section 3). No Hausa, Edo or other-language word is planned in this game, so **`Madalla` (Hausa) and `Kóyo` (Edo) are dropped from this game's results-screen pool**: the akoni-dike results pool is `Ẹ kú iṣẹ́!` (yo) and `Ọ dị mma!` (ig) plus Pidgin (`no-wahala`, `oya`), each `_review`. (The history is Yoruba and Igbo by design; part 6 Principle 3 records the honest gap.) If the owner wants the wider pool back, `Madalla` and `Kóyo` return with a native Hausa or Edo review tag; developer request: the results pool for this game is a per-game list, not the shared rotation.

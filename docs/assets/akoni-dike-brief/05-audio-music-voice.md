# Akọni & Dike brief, part 5: SFX, music, voice and language

Binding: `CULTURAL_GUIDE.md` sections 3 to 5, `ASSET_BIBLE.md` "Audio", `STYLE_GUIDE.md` section 9 (SFX rules: attack 2 to 5 ms, exponential release, peak <= -6 dBFS, +/-2% pitch and +/-1 dB random per trigger, soft and full mixes, **no generic blip, coin or whoosh**). Cues are <= 300 ms; fanfare-class cues (marked **L**) are <= 1.5 s. Code lives in `games/akoni-dike/src/audio/sfx.ts` (`akoniSfx`) and `games/akoni-dike/src/audio/instruments-akoni.ts` (new voices, registered with `audio.registerInstruments`, so **core is not edited**). Register `{ ...coreSfx, ...akoniSfx }`; soft mix via `softSfx`.

## 1. Sound identity per campaign

The sound sets differ on purpose so each campaign sounds like its people: **Kiriji (Yoruba) = talking drum (gángan, dùndún), agogo, shekere**; **Ekumeku (Igbo) = ogene, udu, ekwe**; **Prologue (Ife legend) = shekere crowd, torch, soft drum**. Cues with a campaign variant carry a suffix `K` or `E`; the game picks by campaign. No violence uses a flesh or blood sound: a hit is wood and drum.

## 4. Music brief (commissioned; silent placeholders until delivered)

Rules (as `MUSIC_BRIEF.md`): original compositions only, work-for-hire or fully licensed for web-game embedding; instruments Nigerian and live or clearly licensed; **instrumental only**; no samples of existing songs; no artist sound-alikes; no pan-African "savanna" bed; no AI music of unknown licence. `.ogg` plus `.mp3`, -16 LUFS, seamless. **Size:** `MUSIC_BRIEF.md` asks 30 to 60 s at 96 kbps, which is 360 to 720 KB and cannot meet the 300 KB rule; this game asks **24 to 33 s loops at <= 64 kbps joint stereo (about 200 to 265 KB)**. Leave 800 Hz to 2.5 kHz clear on cue beats so `tell`, `parry` and `padPerfect` stay audible. Duck music 6 dB under barks. ### 4.1 Licensed-music option (note only)

Commissioned music is the plan of record and ships first. **Do not rely on licensed songs.** Licensed tracks may be added later behind a build flag (`features.licensedMusic`, default off) only after **written clearance that explicitly covers web-game use**; until that clearance is filed, no licensed audio enters the repository, and every track slot keeps its original `akd-*` file as the fallback. The clearance, rights holder and date go into `A/CREDITS.md` before the flag is ever enabled. This brief names no artist and no title.

## 5. Voice barks and dialogue

**All story text is read on screen, not voiced** `[AB]`. Only short battle and stealth barks are recorded: <= 1.2 s, three takes (neutral, brighter, softer), mono 44.1 kHz WAV master, delivered as `.ogg` and `.mp3` at 48 kbps, peak -3 dBFS, 150 ms room tone at the head. Barks are <= 150 KB per campaign (part 4). One voice at a time; music ducks 6 dB. Recorded by **Nigerian voice actors; Yoruba and Igbo lines by native speakers who also confirm spelling and tone marks; never text-to-speech, never a faked or exaggerated accent**, no "villager" voices. The rival side speaks in the same register as the heroes, never mocking. **Company constables speak plain English in a neutral voice with no ethnic accent signal** `[SC 3]`. Licence (buyout, with a no-AI-training clause welcome) and actor go in `CREDITS.md`.
